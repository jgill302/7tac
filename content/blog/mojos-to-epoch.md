# From Mojo’s on Guad to a Table at Epoch

A city can carry the memory of a closed coffeehouse in something as small as a drink order.

Mojo’s Daily Grind was on Guadalupe, part of the Austin coffeehouse history we want to keep talking about. In a 2016 profile, Austin.com described Epoch’s Iced Mojo as a nod to the former shop, which had been run by friends on Guadalupe. The same piece traced Epoch’s beginnings to 2006 and three local baristas: Kevin Garry, Joe Rodriguez, and Chris Clarkson. The connection runs through people as well as a name on a menu. [Read Dani Dudek’s Epoch profile](https://austin.com/its-easy-to-see-why-we-love-epoch-coffee-for-keeping-austin-caffeinated/).

The Iced Mojo also appears on Epoch’s archived Village menu, a small record of that continuity. Ask the baristas what they recommend when you visit. [See the archived Epoch menu](https://epochcoffee.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/Village-Menu.pdf).

At 7TAC, our respect for Epoch is personal. Our team often works from there. We appreciate having somewhere to sit with an idea, talk it through, and keep working alongside other people going about their day. If you recognize us at a table, come say hi. We’d be glad to meet you.

That invitation comes with a little coffeehouse etiquette: order something, look after your table, and be considerate about how much space you occupy. The staff are working, other people may be studying, and a familiar room stays welcoming through many small acts of care.

Support Epoch and the other independent cafés, makers, and neighborhood businesses you want to keep around. Buy what you can, tip when you can, credit the people doing the work, and bring a friend back. The places we miss once they’re gone need our attention while they’re here.
